## Change in extraction, not another signature check

The old route thins the mask, merges junction zones, moves representative
vertices, prunes and smooths. Equal Betti numbers at the end do not certify that
those operations preserve the embedded solid. The new opt-in cubical_retract
starts from the union of the *closed occupied voxel cells*. All faces belong
to the complex. A pair is removed only when the lower cell is a codimension-one
free face of a maximal cell. Its full sequence can be independently replayed
from the caller's original mask and physical grid boundaries.

An elementary free-face collapse supplies a strong deformation retraction.
Composition therefore gives a constructive retraction of this voxel complex
onto the final subcomplex. If the final complex is one-dimensional, its graph
is exported with every actual cubical edge retained in its polylines. Suppressing
degree-two vertices changes only graph representation, not the embedded set.
No RDP, endpoint motion, layout algorithm or abstract-polynomial fallback occurs.
Isolated components survive. Partial reductions are retained, never treated as
proof that no spine exists and never forced to a point or graph.

This is not a new theorem about cubical collapse. It implements a stronger
checkable correspondence than homology matching for a specified discrete solid.
The construction is standard; see the DGtal cubical-complex documentation on
free-pair collapse (https://www.dgtal.org/doc/1.4/moduleCubicalComplex.html).

## Boundary convention

sample_grid_breaks realizes samples as dual voxel cells, clipping the first and
last cells exactly at the supplied finite-domain endpoints. Touching an outer
face is not automatically failure for *this explicitly finite-box object*.
Opposite faces are NOT glued, and no empty padding is interpreted as an added
physical region. This does not justify calling the result a periodic
Brillouin-zone invariant. The earlier Hamiltonian notebook and pilot remain
unchanged while the separate finite-box path is evaluated.

## What remains to connect

A voxel collapse is not a proof that the continuous source solid is represented
by that voxel complex. That analytic-to-discrete correspondence must be supplied
separately. The existing field-continuation certificate compares analytic solids
at different parameters; it does not fill this gap. Neither certificate proves
that a legacy extracted graph was correct. No automatic production replacement
or topology-class merger is enabled by this experiment.

For TPMS, continuation identities may join parameter locations only after the
same analytic fields, sphere and coordinate conventions are matched. Different
certified components are not automatically different phases. No label filtering
or removal of unresolved cells is allowed.
