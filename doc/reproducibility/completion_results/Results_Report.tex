\section*{1. Computations and the objects they describe}
A parameterized solid and a selected graph extracted from that solid carry different equivalence questions. We therefore retain three separate objects: the analytic sublevel set, its finite closed-voxel realization, and the embedded graph obtained by checked elementary collapses. Polynomial values label the selected embedded graphs; regularity certificates describe continuation of the analytic solids. Neither object is silently substituted for the other.

The complete Hamiltonian scan contains 7,140 points at $120^3$, with 60 interpolation samples including both endpoints. The spacing is $1/59$. The five energy-prefix lengths are 16, 24, 16, 24 and 39, taken from \texttt{linspace(0.30,5.25,50)}. The three TPMS grids contain 1,323 points at the same voxel resolution, retaining $\lambda\in\texttt{linspace(0,1,21)}$ and $c\in\texttt{linspace(0,0.3,21)}$.

Every originally timed-out noncavity graph was retried without changing its vertices or polyline points. Forty generic projections were ranked by crossing count before bounded exact evaluation. Two distinct completed projections were required to agree for a subcubic spatial value; higher-valence results remain fixed-diagram values. The table includes all original grid points and the accepted retry outcomes.

The cavity entries were recognized but not recomputed through a new treatment. They are not failed spatial evaluations and are not filled with abstract graph polynomials. An uncompleted value remains unavailable. All original records and unsuccessful attempts are retained in the data package.

\subsection*{Analytic event evidence}
The adaptive analytic calculation partitions the three original parameter domains into \RegularLeaves{} certified regular rectangles and 1,479 initially unresolved rectangles. Local critical-point certificates, exact stationary branches and the subsequent level-resolved system now establish event existence in \EventCells{} of the initially unresolved rectangles. The remaining \UnknownCells{} rectangles are unknown. These are counts of rectangles, not counts of distinct critical points, curves or topology-changing transitions.

\begin{center}\input{table_events}\end{center}

The area columns use exact rational arithmetic on the stored binary parameter endpoints before decimal reporting. An event-containing rectangle can contain mostly regular points: its area is not the area of the critical set. The connected components of the certified regular cover provide positive continuation relations, not a proof that separate components are inequivalent.

\section*{4. Analytic continuation and critical-event certification}
\begin{figure}[h!]
\centering\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{Figures/PorousMaterialContinuationMap.pdf}
\caption{A separate analytic-continuation map in the same presentation. Colors label connected components of the certified regular-rectangle cover, locally within each family. Gray rectangles contain a proved stationary event; white rectangles remain unresolved. Distinct colors are not asserted to be distinct ambient-isotopy classes. The lower geometries are the same retained voxel/graph samples as in the spatial-signature figure; displaying them beside analytic certificates does not itself prove analytic-to-voxel correspondence.}
\end{figure}
The first event search fixed $\lambda$ and enclosed the resulting critical level. The additional calculation instead fixes a level inside the requested rectangle and treats $\lambda$ as an unknown. For an interior event it solves
\begin{align}
\nabla h_\lambda(x,y,z)&=0,& h_\lambda(x,y,z)-c&=0.
\end{align}
On the spherical wall, the square system is
\begin{align}
\nabla h_\lambda-\mu(x,y,z)&=0,& x^2+y^2+z^2-R^2&=0,& h_\lambda-c&=0.
\end{align}
Numerical roots propose boxes only. For each accepted box, both interval backends recompute a strict Krawczyk inclusion and a contraction bound below one. The entire $\lambda$ enclosure must lie inside the query rectangle, the fixed level must lie inside its level interval, and a bulk box must lie strictly inside the ball. The new method certified \NewEvents{} further rectangles without relaxing these acceptance conditions.

\section*{5. What the voxel audit establishes}
A deformation retract can preserve homotopy while the original voxel object has singular local neighborhoods. We therefore tested every lattice-vertex link of every original sampled mask. The occupied octants form a triangular subcomplex of an octahedral sphere. An interior manifold vertex has the complete sphere as its link; a boundary manifold vertex has a disk. The implementation checks incidence, connectedness, all link-vertex links, Euler characteristic and the boundary cycle for all 256 octant patterns.

\begin{center}\input{table_links}\end{center}

This is an exact finite incidence test of the chosen closed-voxel realization. Face-sharing cubes pass, whereas cubes meeting only at an edge or a point fail. Disconnected balls and a shell remain valid manifolds with boundary. Passing does not imply that a shell is a handlebody or that a voxel object is ambient-isotopic to its analytic source.

\subsection*{Comparison within a common analytic certificate}
The package also searches for pairs of original TPMS sample points lying in one certified regular analytic rectangle. For every retained example it freshly replays that rectangle's complete regularity certificate with both interval backends and retains both sampled sources and available graph witnesses. Up to twelve examples of each type are retained: \HomologyConflicts{} digital-homology conflicts and \SignatureVariations{} selected-spine signature variations.

When the digital Betti tuples differ inside one such rectangle, the two voxel realizations cannot both recover the homotopy type of their analytically isotopic source solids. A difference of selected-spine polynomials alone has a weaker implication: it demonstrates that this extracted signature is not constant across the certified source family, but does not isolate whether discretization or the choice of spine causes the difference. The exact cases, coordinates and certificates are in \texttt{continuum\_vs\_digital\_witnesses.json}.

\subsection*{The central exact singularity}
For the retained Schwarz-P-to-Diamond field,
\begin{align}
h_\lambda={}&(1-\lambda)(\cos x+\cos y+\cos z)\nonumber\\
&+\lambda(\cos x\cos y\cos z-\sin x\sin y\sin z),
\end{align}
the critical branches $c=1-2\lambda$ at $(\pi,0,0)$ and $c=2\lambda-1$ at $(\pi,\pi,0)$ are exact. At $\lambda=1/2,c=0$, the whole line $(\pi,t,0)$ inside the ball is stationary. A certificate requiring an isolated unique gradient root cannot cover a segment of this line. Exact branch witnesses therefore complement, rather than replace, the local isolated-root calculation.

\subsection*{Claims that remain separate}
Completing the finite parameter scans does not supply a complete classification of analytic solids into distinct ambient-isotopy classes. The regularity cover proves positive continuation within its connected components; event certificates prove existence of stationary events; the voxel collapse and link tests characterize the represented cubical complexes; and normalized subcubic polynomials characterize the selected spatial graphs. A theorem or certificate connecting these different objects must be supplied before stronger identifications are made.
